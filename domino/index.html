How to play

Puerto Rican dominoes (dominó boricua), with double-six tiles and the scoring used on Mesa28.

The table

  • Four players in two teams. Your partner sits across from you. Each player gets 7 tiles, so all 28 are dealt.
  • Play goes counterclockwise: from you, to the player on your right, to your partner, to the player on your left.

Playing

  • First hand: whoever has the double six starts, and must play it.
  • Later hands: the winner of the last hand starts with any tile.
  • Match a tile to either open end. If you can play, you must. If nothing fits, you pass (paso) automatically.
  • Tap a tile to play it. When it fits both ends, tap the glowing spot where you want it.

Ending a hand

  • Dominó: a player plays their last tile. Their team wins the hand.
  • Capicú: the last tile is not a double and fits on either end.
  • Chuchazo: the last tile is the double blank, la chucha.
  • Dominau: the last tile is a double that closes both ends.
  • Tranque: nobody can play. In games to 100 or 200, the team with the fewest points between both players wins. In games to 500, only the player who closed the game and the next player after them count: whoever holds fewer points wins the hand for their team. On a tie, nobody scores and the last hand's winner starts again.

Scoring

  • The winning team scores every pip left in all four hands, theirs included.
  • Games to 500 add prizes: capicú +100, chuchazo +100, and winning the first four hands +100, +75, +50, +25.
  • The first team to reach the target wins the game.

Playing with a friend

  • Pass the phone. Your tiles turn face down after you play, and the next person taps to see theirs.
